**Rule:** NEVER infer launch/go-to-market urgency from development-speed requests. The founder's "speed up development" / "do it now" applies to BUILDING. Serving the product to real people (cutover, D13 spend, MSG91 sends, CSP go-live) is a separate founder decision with its own timing — present it as an available option, not a queued next step.

**Why:** 2026-06-12 — after Wave 6 completed, I framed cutover/launch as imminent ("days away", "say go cutover"). Founder: "am I said anywhere that we are going to serve the product to people this fast?" He never had. Context: no company for ~3 months, incorporation first, Razorpay later. The correct parking position is DEV-COMPLETE: everything built/tested/merged, zero new spend, launch = a button he presses whenever.

**How to apply:** Distinguish three states explicitly in all planning: (1) dev-complete [engineering end], (2) cutover/launch [founder's business call], (3) revenue-on [post-incorporation]. Timelines I present should end at dev-complete unless the founder has explicitly set a launch date. Cost-starting actions (D13 LB, paid services) NEVER dispatch without a fresh founder go at that moment, regardless of earlier in-principle approvals.
